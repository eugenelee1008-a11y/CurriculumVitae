About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Econometrics at Korea University, with research interests in natural language processing (NLP), treatment effect estimation, and machine learning.

My current research focuses on applying computational methods to economic analysis, particularly using NLP to extract meaningful information from unstructured textual data and developing econometric approaches to causal inference. My dissertation research includes applications to public attitudes toward climate change and the evaluation of environmental policies.

Before pursuing my doctoral research, I worked as a researcher at the Korea Development Institute (KDI), where I contributed to policy research on carbon taxation, emissions trading systems, and renewable energy markets.

I am particularly interested in exploring how emerging machine learning techniques can complement traditional econometric methods and broaden the possibilities for empirical economic research.

Job Market Paper

Dissertation · Chapter 2

Difference-in-Differences with Changing Qualification: Theory, Simulation, and Evidence from U.S. NOx Regulation

Uses a Difference-in-differences (DD) framework for sequential policies in which treatment qualification/eligibility can change across policies. The framework distinguishes qualification-related untreated outcome changes from causal policy effects, permits period-specific treatment effects, and examines both conventional conditional parallel trends and deviations from parallel trends.

Monte Carlo simulations examine estimator behaviors under stable versus changing qualification/eligibility, and under alternative untreated-trend assumptions. The empirical application studies U.S. ozone-season Nitrogen Oxides (NOx) emissions under the original Cross-State Air Pollution Rule (CSAPR), the 2017 CSAPR Update, and the 2021 Revised CSAPR Update, with regulatory qualification reconstructed from EPA pre-policy transport assessments and emissions data. The chapter examines how changing state qualification/eligibility can affect interpretation of DD estimates.

Other Dissertation Research

Dissertation · Chapter 1

NLP-Based Analysis of Public Attitudes Toward Climate Change in Korea

Constructs a corpus of approximately 50,000 Korean-language YouTube comments about climate change, carbon neutrality, and environmental policy. Latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), an unsupervised topic model, is used to organize comments into five themes. A KoELECTRA-based Korean-language transformer classifier is then fine-tuned on manually annotated comments to measure negative, neutral, and positive sentiment. The study evaluates context-enhanced classification and video-level validation to measure public attitudes and examine how sentiment differs across climate-policy topics and discussion contexts.

Dissertation · Chapter 3

Difference-in-Differences with Varying Qualification: Untreated Moving Effects and Past-Dependent Treatment Effects

Uses "stayer-based DD" comparisons for qualification/eligibility stayers, and distinguishes untreated movements associated with entering and leaving qualification/eligibility. Uses Monte Carlo simulation to examine estimation bias and treatment-effect heterogeneity due to prior qualification history. Applies this framework to California's "Cap-and-Trade" facility-level greenhouse-gas and local air-pollutant emissions such as Nitrogen Oxides (NOx).
